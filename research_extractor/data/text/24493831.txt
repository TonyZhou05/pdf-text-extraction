Anti-therapeutic antibody testing
Serum samples for evaluating anti-therapeutic antibodies (ATAs) to
onartuzumab were collected before dosing at each treatment cycle and at the study
termination visit. The samples were assayed in a validated bridging
electrochemiluminescence assay. The conjugate reagents (onartuzumab conjugated
with biotin and onartuzumab conjugated with BVTAG™ label [BioVeris Corporation,
Gaithersburg, MD]) were used at an in-plate concentration of 1.0 µg/mL. Diluted
serum samples and conjugates were incubated together. Streptavidin-coated
magnetic beads (BioVeris Corporation) were added to the plates before reading on a
BioVeris M384 analyzer (BioVeris Corporation).
The assay cut-point was determined from a set of 100 onartuzumab-naïve
individual human cancer sera samples by defining the signal below which no specific
antibody against onartuzumab could be detected by the assay. The assay cut-point
was set to have an estimated false-positive rate of 5%. The relative sensitivity of the
assay was estimated to be 143 ng/mL using affinity-purified polyclonal cynomolgus
monkey anti-onartuzumab antibodies and the assay could detect 500 ng/mL of the
antibodies in the presence of 50 μg/mL onartuzumab.
All study samples were initially screened at the minimum dilution of the assay
(1/25). Samples with a response below the cut-point in the initial screen were
determined to be negative. A positive response above the cut-point in the initial
screen was confirmed to be specific to onartuzumab by competitive binding

(immunodepletion) with a large molar excess (100 µg/mL) of onartuzumab. Samples
that did not immunodeplete were determined to be negative for ATA.
All study samples determined to be positive by immunodepletion with
onartuzumab were further characterized to determine if the ATA response was
primarily towards the complementarity-determining region (CDR), engineered
framework, or non-engineered framework of onartuzumab by competitive binding
(immunodepletion) with a large molar excess (100 µg/mL) of the following: (1) an
antibody identical to onartuzumab, but with a different CDR, and (2) an antibody with
the same CDR, but a non-engineered framework. All samples positive by
immunodepletion with onartuzumab were also titrated. The titer was calculated and
reported as log of the inverse of the sample dilution at which the sample response

would equal the assay cut-point. The minimum dilution of a sample was 1/25,
therefore, the minimum detectable response for the assay was 1.4 titer units.

Human serum MET electrochemiluminescence assay
Meso Scale Discovery (MSD; Gaithersburg, MD) microtiter plates were coated
with goat anti-human HGF receptor affinity-purified polyclonal antibodies (R&D systems)
at 6 mg/mL to capture MET. Diluted samples, standards and controls were added to the
plate and incubated. Subsequently, ruthenium conjugated to mouse anti-human MET
monoclonal antibody 1928 (Genentech Inc., CA) was added for detection and incubated.
A tripropylamine-containing read buffer was added for iluminescence. The plates were
read on the MSD Sector Imager 6000.
Pharmacokinetic analysis
Diluted serum samples were assayed for onartuzumab and bevacizumab
concentrations using validated sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays. In
the onartuzumab assay, a murine anti-onartuzumab CDR monoclonal antibody
(Genentech, Inc., South San Francisco, CA) was used as the capture reagent and
goat anti-human immunoglobulin (Ig)G antibodies Fc-conjugated to horseradish
peroxidase (HRP) were added for detection. The minimum dilution of samples was
1/200 and the minimum quantifiable concentration (MQC) was 200 ng/mL. In the
bevacizumab assay, full-length recombinant human VEGF (Genentech, Inc., South
San Francisco, CA) was used as the capture reagent and goat anti-human IgG
antibodies Fc-conjugated with HRP were added for detection. The minimum dilution
of samples was 1/100 and the MQC was 78 ng/mL.
