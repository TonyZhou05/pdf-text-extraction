49 Translational Relevance:
50
51 Overcoming platinum resistance in ovarian cancer (PROC) is an unmet medical
52 need. There is preclinical evidence showing that platinum resistance is AKT kinase -
53 mediated. In this Phase IB study, the AKT kinase inhibitor afuresertib was combined
54 with 3-weekly paclitaxel and carboplatin in patients with PROC. The combination
55 was tolerable with rash defining the maximum tolerated dose of 125 mg/day of
56 afuresertib. An overall RECIST (v1.1) response rate of 32% with a progression free
57 survival of 7.1 months was observed. This compares favorably to a historical
58 response rate of <15% when patients with platinum-resistance are re-exposed to
59 platinum-containing treatments. Our findings indicate that the combination of an AKT
60 kinase inhibitor with platinum-based chemotherapy is effective and durable and
61 support the preclinical hypothesis that AKT kinase contributes to platinum resistance.
62 Further clinical evaluation of this combination is warranted.

73 Abstract
74 Purpose: Preclinically, AKT kinase inhibition restores drug sensitivity in platinum-
75 resistant tumors. Here the pan-AKT kinase inhibitor afuresertib was given in
76 combination with paclitaxel and carboplatin (PC) in patients with recurrent platinum-
77 resistant epithelial ovarian cancer (PROC) and primary platinum refractory ovarian
78 cancer (PPROC).
79 Experimental Design: Part I was a combination 3+3 dose-escalation study for
80 recurrent ovarian cancer. Patients received daily continuous oral afuresertib at 50–
81 150 mg/day with three-weekly intravenous paclitaxel (175 mg/m2) and carboplatin
82 (AUC5) for 6 cycles followed by maintenance afuresertib at 125mg/day until
83 progression or toxicity. Part II was a single arm evaluation of the clinical activity of
84 this combination in recurrent PROC (Cohort A) or PPROC (Cohort B). Patients
85 received oral afuresertib at the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) defined in Part I in
86 combination with PC for 6 cycles, followed by maintenance afuresertib. Primary

93 identified as 125 mg/day. The most common (≥ 50%) drug-related adverse events
94 observed in Part I of the study were nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, alopecia, fatigue and
95 neutropenia and, in Part II, were diarrhea, fatigue, nausea and alopecia. The Part II

118 Introduction
119 The genetic and molecular mechanisms that determine resistance to platinum-based
120 chemotherapy in epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) have yet to be fully expounded.
121 Nonetheless, analysis of the molecular pathways represented in sub-clones of
122 resistant ovarian cancer cells reveals significant molecular signalling alterations
123 compared to chemotherapy-naïve disease [1]. In high-grade serous EOC, over-
124 expression and copy number alterations in components of the phosphoinositide 3-
125 kinase (PI3K)/ Protein kinase B (AKT)/mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1
126 (mTORC1) pathway are common (~46%) but the cascade is also a driver of
127 treatment-resistance [2,3]. In resistant cells, exposure to DNA damaging agents has
128 been shown to activate AKT and anti-apoptotic signaling. Various hypotheses have
129 been proposed to explain the role played by AKT in resistance, including its
130 phosphorylation and activation by the non-homologous end joining repair protein
131 DNA-dependent protein kinase, catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs/PRKDC) [4]. Inhibitors
132 of the upstream kinase mTORC1 have been shown to reverse resistance, but the
133 effect is short-lived due to feedback upregulation of AKT [5]. Following xenograft
134 evidence that inhibition of AKT restores platinum sensitivity in clinically-acquired
135 platinum-resistant tumor cells, a small study of a pan-AKT inhibitor in patients with
136 gynecologic cancers yielded encouraging results [6].
137 Afuresertib (GSK2110183, ASB183) is an orally bioavailable, low-nanomolar, ATP-
138 competitive, reversible inhibitor of all three AKT kinase isoforms (AKT1‒3) that
139 induces significant growth delay in human tumor xenograft models. When given as
140 monotherapy in a first-in-human hematologic study afuresertib displayed evidence of

143 The overall aim of our study was to determine whether the preclinically-

153 at a dose of 125mg/day until disease progression or the emergence of unacceptable

166 Patients and Methods
167 This open-label, multicenter escalation/ expansion study was conducted at 10 clinical
168 centers across 3 countries (United Kingdom, Australia, and Russia). This study was
169 performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the principles of Good
170 Clinical Practice. The protocol was approved by Institutional Review Boards within
171 each country, and all patients provided written informed consent before undergoing
172 any study procedures.
173 The primary objectives of the Part I /dose escalation were to determine the safety
174 and tolerability of afuresertib administered in combination with PC in patients with
175 recurrent PROC (to define the MTD), and to identify the optimal combination dosing
176 regimen to be evaluated in the expansion phase. In the Part II expansion, the
177 primary objective was to confirm safety and evaluate any clinical efficacy signal
178 (investigator-assessed ORR as per RECIST v1.1) of afuresertib given at the MTD in
179 relapsed PROC or PPROC EOC [8]. Secondary endpoints were clinical efficacy,
180 defined as response rate by Gynecological Cancer Intergroup (GCIG) cancer antigen
181 125 (CA125) criteria [9] and progression free survival (PFS) per RECIST v1.1.

185 termed “ovarian cancer” or EOC), adequate organ function, no peripheral neuropathy
186 ≥ grade 2 and no history of type I (or recent diagnosis of type II) diabetes. Patients
187 must have had (RECIST v1.1 or GCIG CA125 criteria-defined) disease progression

364 Discussion
365 To address the preclinical evidence that pan-AKT kinase inhibitors are capable of
366 overcoming platinum-resistance, we gave afuresertib with PC chemotherapy at a
367 dose and schedule (175mg/m2 paclitaxel and AUC5 carboplatin, given 3-weekly for 6
368 cycles) used for the front-line management of EOC. This chemotherapy schedule
369 was selected as a backbone for afuresertib so it could be later evaluated in the
370 upfront setting. Rash defined the MTD of afuresertib as 125mg/day, the same MTD
371 dose as was derived in the first-in-human hematologic study [7]. There was a higher
372 toxicity burden in our combination study than was described in the first-in-human
373 afuresertib trial, with all patients experiencing at least one AE. Some side effects
374 such as alopecia, neutropenia, neuropathy and arthralgia were likely to be have
375 been caused by the PC chemotherapy backbone, particularly the paclitaxel
376 component. However, the combination was generally well-tolerated with
377 approximately two-thirds of patients completing the six-cycle course of treatment and
378 remaining on study for a median duration that exceeded 6 months.

380 Although there are no reference studies in which 3-weekly PC has been used in
381 PROC, in one study of patients of a similar age and median platinum-free interval,
382 RECIST response rates of approximately 13% were observed on re-exposure to
383 platinum-based treatment [13]. Here, we demonstrated a confirmed ORR of 32% by
384 RECIST v1.1 and 52% by CA125 criteria respectively. This is significantly better than
385 would be expected in this resistant population of patients particularly within the
386 context of the imaging schedule we utilized; although it should be noted that our
387 hypothesized ORR of 40% was not achieved. We saw a clinical benefit rate (sum of
388 CR, PR and SD) of 71% and responses were durable with a median PFS of 7.1
389 months. However, it is important to caution that this study was small and response
390 ranges were wide, suggesting that the combination treatment was more effective in
391 some patients than others.
392 Our study had limitations. A larger dose expansion cohort would have more clearly
393 characterized disease response. PPROC comprises a small subset (~10%) of EOC
394 patients with a dismal survival outcome [14]. The scarcity of these patients meant
395 that the PPROC-only cohort failed to recruit and was eventually closed. However, it
396 is noteworthy that, with the addition of a patient in Part I, there were 3 patients with
397 PPROC recruited to this study and, among them, one partial response was
398 observed.
399 In accordance with standard of care, tumor assessments were scheduled after every

405 As encouraging activity signals had been observed in an earlier monotherapy study

410 administered concurrent with chemotherapy.
411 At the time of this study, BRCA1/2 gene testing was only approved for EOC patients
412 with an indicative familial or personal cancer history and was therefore unknown for
413 the majority of our trial participants. As recent in vitro work has shown that levels of
414 AKT kinase are upregulated in BRCA-mutant ovarian cancer cells and AKT kinase
415 inhibition enhances cisplatin-induced DNA damage repair [17], it is possible that
416 some of the efficacy signal observed in our study was in patients with impaired
417 germline or somatic BRCA function. The lack of pharmacodynamic endpoints meant
418 that we missed an opportunity to retrospectively assess BRCA or identify molecular
419 markers of activity (such as changes in tumor AKT kinase) that could be used for
420 future patient selection.
421 As emerging in vitro data support the role of AKT kinase in mediating platinum
422 resistance, our findings would warrant further investigation. The high rate of
423 responses observed in the platinum-resistant patients supports the hypothesis that
424 AKT kinase inhibitors could overcome chemotherapy resistance. Overall this study
425 presents intriguing evidence that AKT kinase inhibition in combination with
426 chemotherapy could be effective in the treatment of platinum-resistant ovarian
427 cancer.
