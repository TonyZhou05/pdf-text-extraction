Introduction: This trial was designed to determine the maximum tolerated dose of
intravenous daunorubicin (DNR) in combination with valspodar and to test the feasibility
of P-glycoprotein modulation using valspodar in elderly patients with previously untreated
acute myelogenous leukemia receiving standard induction chemotherapy.
Methods: Patients 560 years of age with previously untreated AML received valspodar
(10 mg/kg/24 h by continuous intravenous infusion [CIV] on days 1±4 with a 2-mg/kg
loading dose on day 1) in conjunction with two cycles of induction chemotherapy consisting
of cytarabine (200 mg/m2 CIV on days 1±7), and DNR (35 mg/m2 [cohort 1] or 45 mg/m2
[cohort 2] on days 1±3, intravenous bolus). Patients were assessed for dose-limiting toxicities
(DLT), response rate, event-free and overall survival, and pharmacokinetics of valspodar and
DNR.
Results: Valspodar was well tolerated at the lower DNR dose level (ie, 35 mg/m2) resulting
in a 21% rate of DLT and only three toxic deaths. Treatment-related mortality was
unacceptably high at the 45 mg/m2 DNR dose level. The complete response rate was 49%
overall and similar in both cohorts. The median overall survival of patients was 333 days in
cohort 1 compared to 98 days in cohort 2. At baseline, 70% of assessable patients were P-
glycoprotein positive.
Conclusion: Substantial inhibition of P-glycoprotein activity can be achieved in this patient
populationatclinicallytolerabledosesofvalspodarandDNR.Themaximumtolerateddoseof
DNR was established as35 mg/m2.This regimen is beingfurther evaluated in phase III trials.
