Abstract
Background.—Vincristine sulfate liposome injection (VSLI; Marqibo®) is an encapsulated
preparation of standard vincristine in sphingomyelin/cholesterol liposomes. Clinical trials in adults
have demonstrated safety, tolerability, and activity, leading to Food and Drug Administration
(FDA) approval for adults with relapsed acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). Pediatric
experience with VSLI is limited.
Procedure.—This single center, phase I dose escalation study examined the safety, toxicity,
maximum tolerated dose, and pharmacokinetics of VSLI administered weekly to pediatric patients
age <21 years with relapsed or chemotherapy-refractory solid tumors or leukemia.
Results.—Twenty-one subjects were treated in total. Median age was 13.3 years (range 2–19).
Fourteen subjects completed one 28-day cycle of therapy and five subjects completed more than
one cycle. No subject experienced dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) at the first dose level (1.75 mg/m2/
dose, dose range: 2–3.7 mg). At the second dose level (2.25 mg/m2/dose, dose range: 1.3–4.5 mg),
one subject had transient dose-limiting grade 4 transaminase elevation, and this dose level was
expanded with no additional DLT observed. The second dose level then opened to an expansion
phase to evaluate activity in ALL. Clinical activity included minimal residual disease negative

INTRODUCTION
Vincristine sulfate is a highly active agent in pediatric cancers but is associated with acute
and long-term peripheral neuropathy.[1] While chemotherapy dose intensification has
improved outcomes in pediatric malignancies,[2–4] vincristine intensification leads to
increased neuropathy.[5] A trial intensifying vincristine conducted through the Children’s
Oncology Group Trial AALL0433 was terminated early due to excessive peripheral
neuropathy in the intensification arm, especially in older children.[6] To reduce the risk of
severe neurotoxicity, individual doses of vincristine are capped based (e.g., a standard
vincristine dose is 1.5 mg/m2/dose to a maximum of 2 mg/dose). This limitation is
particularly relevant to adolescents and young adults with cancer whose outcomes are
generally worse than younger patients.[7] In this group, risk-adjusted chemotherapy
intensification has the potential to improve outcomes.[8,9]
Vincristine sulfate liposome injection (VSLI; Marqibo®) is composed of standard vincristine
encapsulated in sphingomyelin/cholesterol liposomes. This formulation was designed to
increase the therapeutic index of vincristine, prolonging the half-life and increasing
preferential tissue penetration to sites of disease relative to normal tissues.[10–12] Notably,
these properties obviate the need for dose capping, thus allowing vincristine dose
intensification.
VSLI has been well studied in adults with relapsed or chemotherapy-refractory cancer, with
promising results.[13–15] Results from a pivotal multicenter, singleagent phase II study of
VSLI in adults with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) demonstrated a 20% complete
response rate in heavily pretreated patients with relapsed/refractory disease, all of whom had
prior vincristine exposure.[13] These results led to accelerated approval by the U.S. Food
and Drug Administration (FDA) of VSLI at a dose of 2.25 mg/m2/dose given weekly for use
in adults with relapsed ALL.[16,17] Combination chemotherapy trials in adults have
demonstrated the safety and efficacy of utilizing VSLI in place of standard vincristine.[18]
Only one prior study of VSLI has been performed in pediatrics, the results of which were
not published and complete pharmacokinetic (PK) data were limited to two patients.[19]
This phase I dose escalation trial was conducted to evaluate the safety, toxicity, maximum
tolerated dose (MTD), and PKs of weekly dosing of VSLI in children and adolescents with

PATIENTS AND METHODS
Patients
Pediatric patients (age 2–20 years) with relapsed or chemotherapy refractory malignancies
with measurable or evaluable disease were eligible. Eligibility criteria required that subjects
enroll a minimum of 7 days following chemotherapy and 30 days following investigational
or monoclonal antibody therapy. Subjects with a prior history of stem cell transplantation
were eligible in the absence of active graft-versus-host disease and without
immunosuppressive therapy for at least 4 weeks. Other inclusion criteria included
performance status of >50, adequate liver function with bilirubin <2 × the upper limit of
normal and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) <5 × the
upper limit of normal, and normal serum creatinine for age. A neutrophil count ≥ 1,000/μl
and platelet count ≥50,000/μl were required unless due to marrow involvement by disease.
Subjects with preexisting ≥ grade 2 sensory or motor neuropathies, history of persistent ≥
grade 2 active neurologic disorders unrelated to chemotherapy, active central nervous system
leukemia or lymphoma, or systemic uncontrolled infection were excluded. The use of azole-
based antifungal agents was prohibited due to drug interactions. This protocol was reviewed
and approved by the National Cancer Institute (NCI) institutional review board. Informed
consent, or parental permission with child assent, was obtained in patients in accordance
with institutional guidelines.
Study Design and Treatment
This was a single center, phase I dose escalation study sponsored by Spectrum
Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (http://www.clinicaltrials.gov/show/NCT01222780). A rolling-six
design was employed for subject accrual.[20] Subjects received once per week VSLI
monotherapy based on an actual body surface area and without dose capping for a total of
four doses per cycle. All subjects who received at least one dose of VSLI were evaluable for
toxicity; however, subjects who did not complete at least one full cycle (four doses) for
reasons other than dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) were not evaluable for determination of the
MTD. Such patients were replaced until a cohort with at least three evaluable subjects was
completed.
All VSLI doses were prepared and administered in accordance with the product prescribing
information (http://www.marqibo.com/). The starting dose was 1.75 mg/m2/dose (80% of
the adult MTD). Dose level 2 was 2.25 mg/m2/dose. VSLI dosing was based on the
vincristine component of the drug. VSLI was administered intravenously via peripheral or
central venous access over 60 min on days 1, 8, 15, and 22 of a 28-day course in an
outpatient or inpatient treatment setting. Subjects continued dosing for a full treatment cycle
(four doses) unless there was evidence of disease progression or DLT. In the absence of DLT
and with evidence clinical response, additional cycles of therapy were administered. Dose

Dose-Limiting Toxicity
Toxicity was graded according to the NCI Common Toxicity Criteria for Adverse Events
(Version 4.0). Nonhematologic DLT was defined as any grade 3–5 toxicity related to VSLI
occurring during the first cycle, with exceptions for medically manageable grade 3 toxicities
(e.g., low electrolyte levels). Hematologic DLT was defined by any grade 4 toxicity lasting
greater than 5 days or for which transfusion support was required or by the failure to recover
a neutrophil count to ≥ 1,000/μl or a baseline platelet count by day 35 of the treatment cycle.
Grade 4 neutropenia lasting <1 week was not considered DLT. Subjects with abnormal blood
counts (grade 1 through 4) at baseline due to marrow involvement were not evaluable for
hematologic toxicity. Toxicity data from the first treatment cycle only were used in the MTD
determination.
Pharmacokinetics
Detailed PKs were obtained on each subject as feasible, with all samples drawn from a line
separate from that used for drug infusion. Samples were collected during cycle 1 before the
start of the first infusion, 5 min postinfusion, and at 1, 3, 6, 10, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, and 144
hr (predose #2 trough level). Following blood collection into ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid
(EDTA) tubes, samples were placed on ice immediately and stored in a refrigerator until
processing by separating plasma by centrifugation for 15 min at 10°C following validated
procedures. A validated high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) tandem mass
spectrometry assay with a range of 40 ng/ml (lower limit of quantification) to 10,000 ng/ml
was used to quantitate total (liposomal encapsulated, nonencapsulated, and protein bound)
vincristine (see detailed method in Supplementary Text S1).

Evaluation of Clinical Activity
Standard response criteria were employed.[21–23] All subjects had baseline disease
assessment within 14 days prior to the first dose of VSLI. In subjects with ALL, evaluation
included cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis, bone marrow aspirate/biopsy for morphology,
flow cytometry, and monitoring of peripheral blasts when present. In subjects with solid
tumors or extramedullary leukemia involvement, disease-specific imaging consisted of
computed tomography and/or fludeoxyglucose-positron emission tomography. Postcycle
evaluation was performed at day 28 (± 3 days) of each cycle. Subjects who completed one
full cycle or discontinued therapy early for disease progression were evaluable for response.
RESULTS
Patient, Disease, and Treatment Characteristics
From July 2011 through March 2014, a total of 22 subjects were enrolled, of which 21
subjects received at least one dose of VSLI. The median subject age was 13.3 years, with a
range from 2 to 19 years. Eighteen subjects had relapsed ALL and one each had a diagnosis
of Burkitt lymphoma, alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, desmoplastic small round cell tumor,
and Ewing sarcoma. All subjects were heavily pretreated and had received a median of four
prior regimens (range, 2–8), and all had previously received vincristine. Seven of 18 (39%)
subjects with ALL had undergone a prior allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant
(Table I).
Fourteen subjects completed at least one cycle of therapy and five subjects received more
than one cycle (median, 1; range, 0–3). One subject was enrolled but not treated due to
interval development of ALL-related complications. One patient developed DLT at dose
level 2. Six subjects did not complete a full cycle of VSLI and were removed from the study
for reasons unrelated to VSLI toxicity. This included disease progression (n = 3), alternative
therapy (n = 2), and severe viral respiratory infection (n = 1).
VSLI doses administered to subjects ranged from 1.3 to 4.5 mg/dose (median, 2.9 mg).
Seventeen of 21 (81%) subjects received individual VSLI doses that exceeded the commonly
employed 2 mg maximum for standard vincristine. The median cumulative dose was 10.4
mg (range, 3.4–30 mg) (Table II).
Toxicity
No subject experienced DLT at the first dose level (1.75 mg/m2/dose). At the second dose
level (2.25 mg/m2/dose), one subject had grade 4 AST and ALT elevations after the first
dose that met criteria for DLT and this dose level was expanded to a total of six evaluable
subjects. Notably, this patient had prior history of recurrent grade 3 and 4 hepatic
transaminase elevations related to multiple chemotherapy agents and also had developed
hepatosplenomegaly and grade 3 hepatic transaminase elevation associated with disease
progression immediately prior to starting therapy. No other subjects experienced DLT (Table
III).

Most VSLI-related adverse events were reversible grade 1 and 2 in severity including
transient hepatic transaminase elevations that resolved by the time of subsequent dose
administration, paresthesia, low white blood cell count, neutropenia, and fatigue. Four
subjects evaluable for hematologic toxicity developed grade 4 neutropenia that
spontaneously and rapidly resolved. No subject was taken off study due to neurotoxicity.
One subject met criteria for dose reduction with the third dose in cycle 2 (dose 6) due to
paresthesia. He went on to complete six additional doses of VSLI at the lower dose without
further complication. Fever was frequently observed within 24–48 hr of receiving the first
dose (n = 9, 43%), and acetaminophen prophylaxis was used in these patients for subsequent
dosing. Notably, pyrexia was the most common adverse event seen in the corresponding
prior pediatric experience.[19]
The highest dose level tested was the adult MTD of 2.25 mg/m2. Although the MTD had not
been exceeded at dose level 2, the decision was made to forego further dose escalation and
to open the expansion cohort for subjects with ALL at dose level 2 in accordance with
protocol allowances. This was based on the observed toxicity profile and clinical activity at
dose level 2 (2.25 mg/m2/dose x 4 weekly doses), which also represented the FDA-approved
dose and schedule for adults, and the likelihood that the safety profile would be negatively
impacted by further dose escalation and cumulative dosing. Notably, ongoing dosing of
VSLI appeared to be associated with the development of peripheral neuropathy on this trial,
consistent with results in adults with ALL where cumulative dose was correlated with earlier
onset and higher grade neurotoxicity.[24] Specifically, two subjects who completed at least
one cycle of therapy at the second dose level developed peripheral motor and sensory
neuropathy. In one subject, this was a grade 2 event that would have invoked a treatment
delay had therapy been continued, and in the other subject grade 2 peripheral motor and
sensory neuropathy developed several weeks after the completion of one cycle. Notably,
both of these subjects were teenagers, and higher rates of neurotoxicity have been observed
in older children and adolescents with standard vincristine.[6]
Pharmacokinetics
PK parameters demonstrated wide interpatient variability (Tables II and IV). While an
increase in the maximal VSLI concentration was observed with increasing dose, there was
no increase in the median drug exposure with dose increase. There was also substantial
variability in the terminal half-life and clearance (Figs. 1A and 1B). No relationship was
seen between age and clearance (Fig. 1C) or total drug exposure. A nonstatistically
significant trend was observed in the relationship between age and half-life (Fig. 1D).
Specifically, the median half-life was shorter at 4.85 hr (range: 1.8–40.4 h, n = 8) in those
who were younger than 13 years compared to a median half-life of 16.3 hr (range: 5.9–24.1
hr, n = 11) in those who were 13 and older (P = 0.06). Notably, in the lower age group, one
patient had a remarkably long terminal half-life of 40.4 hr, which far exceeded that observed
in any other subject. This subject also had the highest drug exposure (AUC 77,429 0−∞
ng/ml-hr). Interestingly, this subject had grade 4 hypertriglyceridemia prior to starting VSLI
that was attributed to recent asparaginase therapy.[25] We hypothesized that the
hypertriglyceridemia may have led to delayed clearance and increased exposure. The patient
who developed DLT did not have increased drug exposure (AUC 25,173 ng/ml-hr) in

Clinical Activity
One subject with refractory ALL and posttransplant relapse attained a complete response
that was shown to be minimal residual disease negative by flow cytometry after the second
cycle, who notably also had the highest drug exposure (Fig. 2). Several subjects with ALL
and peripheral blasts were noted to have a dramatic reduction in the absolute peripheral blast
count after a single dose of VSLI (Fig. 2). One subject demonstrated hematologic
improvement and achieved transfusion independence. Nine subjects had stable disease. Four
subjects had progressive disease (Table II).
DISCUSSION
Dose intensification of chemotherapy has led to improved outcomes in pediatric cancer.[2–
5] Intensification of standard vincristine, however, has been limited by neurotoxicity, and the
maximum individual vincristine dose is usually capped at 2 mg/dose and the total number of
weekly doses constrained. The pharmacologic and pharmacodynamic properties of VSLI
would appear to support intensification of vincristine therapy in the treatment of high-risk
pediatric malignancies. [17, 24] Our study provides the first experience administering VSLI
with weekly dosing in children and adolescents following an intensive schedule that is
frequently utilized in pediatric chemotherapy regimens. This phase I study enrolled heavily
pretreated subjects with multiply relapsed and/or chemotherapy-refractory cancers, all of
whom had prior vincristine exposure. Notably, VSLI was well tolerated despite weekly
administration without dose capping, which allowed for significant dose intensification in
comparison to standard vincristine. The median dose of VSLI administered was 2.9 mg
(range, 1.9–4.5 mg) (Table II). Seventeen of 21 (81%) treated subjects received a VSLI dose
that exceeded the 2 mg conventional dose limit set for standard vincristine. Though an MTD
was not reached, based on the observed toxicity profile and the goal to avoid cumulative
toxicity, further dose escalation was not pursued and the recommended phase II dose was
defined as 2.25 mg/m2/dose given once every 7 days, which is the FDA-approved regimen
for adults with ALL.
The PK profile of VSLI in pediatrics demonstrated wide interpatient variability, and no
increase in drug exposure with increasing dose was observed in the small patient sample
studied. The plasma concentration-time curves for VSLI were monoexponential for most
patients (Fig. 1E), in contrast to vincristine, which typically exhibits triexponential curves.
[26, 27] Our assay measured total vincristine (liposomal encapsulated, nonencapsulated, and
protein bound) and thus could not differentiate or provide information about the proportion
of vincristine that was encapsulated versus nonencapsulated. We also observed a trend
toward an association between younger age and shorter half-life, (Fig. 1D) but without any
difference in clearance. Interestingly, the patient (Subject #6) who achieved a complete
response had the highest drug exposure as measured by maximum concentration, area under

A comparison of PK parameters of total vincristine in children to those in adults
demonstrates greater variability and greater median drug exposure in children. At the 2.25
mg/m2 dose level drug exposure in children (median 31,043 ng/ml-hr, range 11,856–63,964
ng/ml-hr) exceeded that in adults (median 13,680 ng/ml-hr, range 7,036–26,074 ng/ml-hr)
by 2.3-fold.[17] The published adult data are not provided in detail, which limits the
comparison. Notably, however, PK samples were processed using the same validated
procedures, so variation in handling does not explain these differences. The sample size for
both studies is small and future investigations in pediatric and adult patients will allow
further characterization of potential differences in the PKs of total vincristine.
Comparative plasma and CSF PKs of vincristine compared to VSLI in our nonhuman
primate model allowed for a direct comparison of vincristine and VSLI PKs and
demonstrated that the clearance of vincristine (33.8 ml/min) exceeded that of VSLI (0.089
ml/min) by approximately 400-fold.[30] Additionally, no penetration into the CSF was
observed for either preparation.[30]
Clinical activity was observed on this single agent trial. Most notably, one subject with
multiply relapsed and chemotherapy refractory ALL who had relapsed after two prior
allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplants attained a minimal residual disease negative
complete response after two cycles of therapy. Additionally, two subjects had dramatic
reductions in peripheral blasts with the first dose of VSLI. Two subjects with solid tumors
had stable disease for two and three cycles of therapy.
The majority of patients did not continue therapy beyond one cycle. Thus, there was limited
ability to assess for cumulative toxicity, specifically for neurotoxicity, which has been the
main limiting factor with vincristine intensification. However, subjects did receive a median
cumulative dose of 10.4 mg (range 3.4–30 mg). Ongoing studies in adults comparing
vincristine to VSLI in randomized studies will be informative.
In conclusion, children and adolescents with relapsed/refractory cancer tolerate VSLI
administered at 2.25 mg/m2/dose weekly x 4, which is also the MTD identified in adults,
with a comparable toxicity profile and single-agent clinical activity. Based on tolerability
and clinical activity, a pilot trial incorporating VSLI as substitution for standard vincristine
with combination chemotherapy in patients with relapsed ALL is planned. The goal is to use
VSLI with combination chemotherapy to allow for dose intensification with the aim to
improve survival outcomes with reduced or comparable neurotoxicity in comparison to
standard vincristine.
