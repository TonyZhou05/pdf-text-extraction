[SourceID: 1] Several chemotherapeutic agents have been proven
effective in treating patients with small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Among them, cisplatin is unique in having a low incidence of myelosupression. This agent thus plays a key role in most of the combination regimens used in treating SCLC and NSCLC. However, because cisplatin demonstrate serious toxicity such as gastrointestinal, renal and neurotoxity in many patients, it can lead to a deterioration in performance status, and thus to the premature discontinuation of treatment.
Recent reports have shown that a new derivative of camptothecin, irinotecan (CPT-11), 7-Ethyl-l0-[4-(1-piperidino)-1-piperidino] carbonyloxy-camptothecin, exerts potent activity against several malignant tumors. In an initial phase II clinical study, CPT-11 produced satisfactory response rates of 34% in patients with previously untreated advanced NSCLC and of 47% in patients with refractory or relapsed SCLC [1,2]. Like the agents paclitaxel, docetaxel, gemcitabine, and vinorelbine, CPT-11 is now being evaluated as a component of new combination regimens [3][4][5].
Ifosfamide (IFM), an alkylating oxazaphosphorine that is similar to cyclophosphamide, is one of the most active agents against SCLC and NSCLC [6,7]. Experiments conducted in vivo with a combination of CPT-11 and cyclophosphamide showed a synergistic effect of these agents, with prolongation of survival time [8]. These results can probably be attributed to different mechanisms of cytocidal effects. Since the toxicity profiles of the two agents differ, their combination may not cause an increase in toxicity. CPT-11 is synergistic when given in combination with other agents, and is effective against pleiotropic drug-resistant tumors [9,10]. We conducted the present phase I study with CPT-11 and IFM to establish a CPT-11-based combination regimen with a relatively low toxicity for the treatment of patients with lung cancer.

[SourceID: 2] Patient population
Patients who were hospitalized at the Kurume University Hospital gave informed consent for entry into the trial. Eligibility criteria included: 1) histologically diagnosed advanced SCLC or NSCLC, except for untreated limited-stage SCLC, which is not evaluated in phase I studies because of the existence of established therapy; 2) 75 years of age or less; 3) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0 to 2; 4) no chemotherapy or radiotherapy administered within the 4 weeks prior to study entry; 5) had not received CPT-11 and/or ifosfamide prior to study; 6) adequate hepatic function (bilirubin level•¬1.5 mg/dL, and transaminase levels •¬2•~the upper limit of normal), adequate renal function (creatinine level•¬1.4 mg/dL), and adequate bone marrow function (as documented by a leukocyte count •¬4,000% L and •¬12,000% L, but a leukocyte count•¬3,000/ƒÊL on days 8,15 and on the day of CPT-11 infusion during first 2 cycles; platelet count•¬100,000/ƒÊL, and hemoglobin concentration •¬ 9.0 g/dL).

[SourceID: 4] Patient characteristics
The characteristics of the patients in this study appear in Table 3. Between August 1995 and January 1997, 27 patients entered the study and completed at least two of the five scheduled cycles of treatment (median, 3; total 82). All 27 patients were assessed for toxicity. There were 22 men and 5 women, median age 64 years (range, 43 to 72 years). Six patients had PS 2. Sixteen patients had NSCLC (adenocarcinoma, n=10; squamous cell carcinoma, n=5; large cell carcinoma, n=1), and 11 had SCLC. Fifteen of the 27 patients had received prior treatment including chemotherapy (cisplatin + etoposide, n=6; cisplatin + UFT, n=1; carboplatin + etoposide, n=1; and cisplatin + carboplatin + etoposide, n=1); radiotherapy alone, n=1; and surgery alone, n=5.

[SourceID: 6] Preliminary observations on efficacy
Although the assessment of efficacy was not primary end point of this study, the response was evaluated in 23 patients with measurable lesions. Six of 14 patients (43%) with NSCLC showed a response to treatment (1 CR and 5 PR). Four of the 6 responses were cases of postoperative recurrence, while two other responses had not previously been treated. Two responses occurred at dose level 2, and one response occurred at dose levels of 3, 4, 5, and 6. Of the 9 patients with SCLC, 7 (78%) exhibited a partial response to treatment.

[SourceID: 7] DISCUSSION
The present study was conducted to determine the feasibility administering CPT-11 in a weekly schedule combined with a fixed dose of IFM in patients with advanced primary lung cancer, SCLC and NSCLC. The MTD of CPT-11 was determined to be 90 mg/m2 on days 1, 8 and 15 given in combination with a fixed dose of IFM 1.5 g/m2 on days 1 through 3. The DLT of this regimen was leukopenia. Therefore, the recommended dose of CPT-11 for a phase II study is 80 mg/m2 on days 1, 8 and 15. We plan to conduct such a phase II study in previously untreated advanced NSCLC.
CPT-11 shows promise in treating lung cancer. In a phase I study, Negoro et al. reported the recommended dose to be 100 mg/m2 administered as a weekly intravenous (IV) infusions [1 1 ], Fukuoka et al. reported that the response rate in a phase II study using this schedule to be 31.9% in patients with previously untreated advanced NSCLC [12]. Masuda et al. performed a phase II study of weekly 100 mg/m2 administered intravenously each week in the treatment of refractory or relapsed SCLC, and found a response rate of 47% [2]. However, the drug also exhibits a unique toxicity profile. The authors previously demonstrated that myelosuppression and unpredictable diarrhea accompanied the administration of 11,12].
A relatively high mortality rate has been reported in patients who received CPT-11 alone or in combination with CDDP. According to Sasaki et al., the dose of CPT-11 showed no significant correlation with the incidence and severity of myelosuppression or diarrhea [13]. Those authors also demonstrated a wide inter-patient variation in the incidence of side effects. Thus, care is required when conducting studies to determine the adequate dose of CPT-11, particularly when that drug is given in combination with other agents.
The present study determined the MTD of CPT-11 given in the first 2 cycles, rather than in just a single cycle, as is typically evaluated in phase I studies. It is well known that the toxic effects of therapy accumulate as the treatment progresses. Therefore, it is probable that the incidence and severity of adverse effects will be underestimated if the MTD is determined in only a single cycle. The relatively high mortality rate that was reported in previous phase II studies of single or combination therapy may have resulted from an incorrect dose of CPT-11.
Masters et al. reported a phase I study of vinorelbine and IFM in patients with advanced NSCLC. They attempted to establish a platinum-free PHASE I STUDY OF CPT-11 AND IPM IN LUNG CANCER regimen in order to reduce toxicity, and observed a response rate of 40% [14]. Hoffman et al. conducted a phase I study of paclitaxel and ifosfamide in patients with advanced NSCLC, and obtained a response rate of 27%. Those authors emphasized that IFM-based chemotherapy was as effective cisplatin based chemotherapy, with less subjective adverse effects and less cumulative toxicity [15] . We also attempted to establish a platinum-free regimen comprised of CPT-11 and IFM, that perhaps might be more effective, and less toxic, than a platinumcontaining regimen. Besides a relatively low level of gastrointestinal toxicity, none of the patients in the present study experienced grade 4 leukopenia, and only one patient developed thrombocytopenia, despite a drug delivery ratio of 90%. This regimen may thus replace platinum-based chemotherapy.
